# ICP comparison — Nudgy's four audiences

Date: 2026-11-16. Compares the four profiles in this folder: [meeting chairs](meeting-chairs.md), [facilitators](facilitators.md), [trainers](trainers.md), [teachers](teachers.md).

## Status of the evidence

- Desk research only. No interviews were held.
- **Unverified.** The research session could run web searches but could not open any page (the network policy blocked page fetches; a re-test by the coordinating session failed the same way). Every quote, price and statistic in the four profiles comes from a search-result excerpt and has not been confirmed against the live page.
- Reddit, LinkedIn and App Store review text were unreachable, so first-person practitioner evidence is thin in all four.
- Nothing in this folder should be quoted in marketing, or treated as settled, until the sources have been opened and checked.
- Sections marked **Reading** below are the coordinator's interpretation of the four profiles, not findings.

## Verdict per audience

| Audience | Fit | Best-fit sub-segment | Main thing in its favour | Main thing against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meeting chairs | Moderate | In-person conference session chairs and panel moderators | Chairing guidelines prescribe warnings at fixed points before the end (3/1/0 or 5/1 minutes), which is the nudge model | No evidence chairs want a private cue; frequency of chairing unknown |
| Facilitators | Moderate | Solo, in-person facilitators of single-block, discussion-led sessions of 60–120 min (support and circle groups, community meetings) | Most work alone at least sometimes, while standard advice relies on a co-facilitator signalling "10, 5, 2 minutes" | Typical session about 90 min; workshop formats use many short timeboxes on a shared visible timer |
| Trainers | Moderate | Independent trainers and speakers delivering in-person timed slots of up to about 60 min | Documented failures of current methods: no visible clock, slides hide the time, phone-checking looks bad | Training blocks run to 90 min and days to 8–9 h; no practitioner found asking for a wrist nudge |
| Teachers | Moderate | Apple Watch-wearing teachers of fixed 40–60 min periods (secondary, college, adult and language courses) | Teachers already improvise this with Siri alarms and the built-in Timer; one asked publicly for alerts 10 and 5 minutes before the end of a 45-minute lecture | The free built-in Timer may be good enough; some UK school policies restrict staff smartwatch use |

No audience came out Strong, and none came out Weak.

## What recurs across all four

1. **The one-hour limit on distinct tap patterns splits every audience.** Inside it: conference talks and session-chair slots, school and college periods, 30-minute private lessons. Outside it: board meetings, typical 90-minute workshops, group-therapy sessions of 60–120 min, 90-minute training blocks, full training days, longer studio classes.
2. **Direct competitors already exist, cheap or free, with few or no ratings.** Named across the profiles: HapticClock (free), Shush ($0.99), TalkPace ($4.99), Haptica ($9.99 lifetime), Tap Timer ($19.99 lifetime), Haptix (free), QuietSession (aimed at therapists and coaches), plus education apps Passing Time ($4.99), Lesson Timer and ClassBell Alarm. The profiles read this as evidence of supply, not of demand.
3. **The same top risk appears three times:** no first-person evidence that the person running the session wants a private cue, as opposed to a shared visible timer (chairs, facilitators) or better session design (trainers). For teachers the equivalent risk is that the built-in Timer is enough.
4. **Apple Watch ownership is a minority filter, and the figures conflict.** The profiles cite 18%, 19% and about 37% of iPhone owners or buyers from different CIRP releases. No figure exists for any of the four professions.
5. **Remote sessions are a poor fit everywhere.** A clock is already on screen.
6. **Per-segment timing is asked for and already offered by others.** A Miro wish-list request wants a facilitator-only nudge at each agenda milestone; Lesson Timer splits a lesson into sections; TalkPace gives per-slide cues. Nudgy has not built agenda segments.
7. **Comparable one-time prices sit between $0.99 and $19.99**, clustering at or below $9.99. No profile recommends a price.
8. **Reliability with the Watch display off is a reported failure in this category** (one App Store review, one developer forum post).

## Reading (coordinator's interpretation)

- The best-fit sub-segments of chairs, trainers and teachers are the same kind of person: **someone alone in front of a room, in person, with a fixed slot of up to about 60 minutes and a hard stop, who cannot look at a clock without it being noticed.** Conference speakers, session chairs, panel moderators and lecturers all match. This crosses the four audience labels rather than sitting inside one.
- Facilitators are the weakest match to the current product: the session-length and multi-timebox problems both apply to most of them.
- Teachers show the only observed workaround behaviour (people already doing this by hand), which is the strongest type of evidence in the set. They also carry the only policy risk.
- If the profiles hold up after verification, three product questions follow: behaviour beyond one hour, starting a session from the Watch without touching the phone, and whether agenda segments move up the list.
