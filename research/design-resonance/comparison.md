# Design resonance across the four ICPs (simulated sessions)

Date: 2026-11-16. Summarises the four session folders beside this file: [meeting-chairs](meeting-chairs/), [facilitators](facilitators/), [trainers](trainers/), [teachers](teachers/).

## What this is and is not

- **Simulated.** No real users took part. Each session had three separate AI agents: one synthetic participant built from the ICP profile, a user research director and a design director. Quotations in the session files are simulated.
- **One participant per audience, one run.** Scores are single ordinal judgements. A difference of one or two points carries no weight.
- **The four sessions are not independent.** All agents run on the same model, and all four sessions reached the same verdict level. Agreement between sessions is weaker evidence than it looks.
- **The personas rest on unverified ICP profiles** (see `research/icp/comparison.md`).
- **Static images only.** Haptics, motion, sound, screen wake and readability on a real wrist were not and could not be judged. The session files say where a participant assumed these.
- The output is a set of hypotheses for real testing. It is not a basis for choosing a direction on its own.

## How each session ran

1. Research director wrote a neutral guide without seeing the designs; design director reviewed all 40 artboards and wrote hypotheses and probes (files `01`, `02`).
2. Participant viewed all 40 artboards and responded (`03`).
3. Both directors asked follow-up questions (`04`, `05`).
4. Participant answered, scored each variant 1–5 on six criteria and ranked all eight (`06`).
5. Research findings and design read-out (`07`, `08`).

Stimulus: the eight variants on the design canvas — A and B originals, A1–A3 and B1–B3 from the studio round — five artboards each.

## What recurred in all four sessions

1. **The two product-led variants led in every session.** A2 and B2 took first and second place in three sessions; in the fourth (teachers) B2 and B3 led with A2 third. The directors attribute this to plain, literal headlines and a native-looking interface, not to dark or light.
2. **Original B was ranked last by all four participants.** Stated causes: the rounded display typeface, the violet-and-yellow pair, and the line "Psst." Three participants read it as a classroom or pupils' app.
3. **Identity work did not register.** A1's mark was not noticed or not decoded. No icon in any variant was read as a timer or as wrist-related. The B and B3 mark was read as a speaker making sound by three participants.
4. **Session screens that show the whole sequence with tap counts in words were clearest:** the tiles on B-Session and B3-Session, the "Then…" line on B2-Session and the labelled bar on A2-Session. Dots or circles without words were not understood (A2-Watch, B1-Session).
5. **"Standard taps only" on the Watch confused every participant.** Two read it as meaning the tap patterns do not work on the Watch.
6. **Every List board lacks a price,** and the Upgrade button gives none. Arrows on locked rows (A2-List) were read as "this opens".
7. **No free timer matches any participant's session.** The 5- and 15-minute timers fit none of: 20-minute speaker slots, 40-minute talks, 50-minute lessons, 90-minute groups. This capped the "for me" scores in all variants and is a product and packaging point, not a design-direction point.
8. **The audience's words differ from the product's.** Participants questioned "nudge", "haptic" and "session"; they said "tap", "time warning", "lesson", "five minutes left".
9. **The reaction was mainly to the product idea and its packaging.** All four research directors concluded that design moved comprehension and setting fit, while the ceiling was set by things identical across all eight variants.

## Where the audiences differed

- **Facilitators** (the bereavement-group persona) preferred the A family and would keep a dark running screen even at the cost of the clearer tiles. The design director's suggested hybrid — A2 ground, B3 tiles, B2 headline — was not shown and is untested.
- **Teachers** preferred the B family once the typeface and colour were calmed (B2, B3). Their weight falls on the Watch screens: the persona's phone stays in a drawer, which is itself a hypothesis.
- **Meeting chairs and trainers** would not choose between A2 and B2 on looks.

## Coordinator's reading (interpretation, not a finding)

- The sessions do not separate direction A from direction B. They separate **plain and native (A2, B2)** from **art-directed (A, A1, A3, B, B1)**. If that holds with real people, the choice between A and B matters less than adopting the A2/B2 treatment in whichever family is chosen.
- The items in "What recurred" numbered 4 to 8 apply to every variant and can be fixed without choosing a direction.
- Original B looks like the one variant with a consistent negative reaction across audiences.

## What real testing must check first

1. Whether one, two and three taps can be told apart on a real Watch while speaking or moving.
2. Whether people reach a usable timer in the free tier, and what a shown one-time price does at the Upgrade button.
3. A2 against B2 with real participants from the candidate primary audience, phone and Watch screens both.
4. Whether the "classroom app" reading of B recurs, and whether typeface, colour or wording causes it.
5. How "Standard taps only" is read, and whether it should be on the Watch at all.
