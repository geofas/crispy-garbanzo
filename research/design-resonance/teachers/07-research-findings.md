# Research findings — teachers (simulated session)

## 1. What this is and is not

One synthetic participant, one run, static images. No real users were involved. The record supports hypotheses about which directions and words may suit a secondary teacher, and where the boards fail to explain themselves. It cannot support claims about real teachers, how common a reaction is, willingness to pay, or anything that depends on haptics, motion or wrist glanceability. The audience profile is unverified desk research.

## 2. Participant

"Hannah", a synthetic secondary-school science teacher: 50-minute periods in a lab, hard stop at the bell.
iPhone and Apple Watch; the phone stays in a drawer in lessons (a hypothesis), so she would start from the Watch.

## 3. What the participant said

All quotations are simulated.

- **First impressions.** Plain headlines won: A2-Web was "the first where I knew what it was at once". A read as office software ("meeting chairs" first, teachers last). B read as "aimed at pupils or a primary classroom", mainly through its typeface: with "Psst" covered it "still reads as made for pupils: fat round letters and bright yellow". A3's light ground was "pleasanter, not mine".
- **Icons.** The B and B3 mark "looks like a speaker making sound, the opposite of silent". A1's orange square "would be the loudest icon on my phone". "None says 'wrist' unaided."
- **Session screens.** Layouts showing the whole sequence were preferred: B's tiles "are where I understood the whole sequence in one look"; B2 was faster than A2 "mainly because it tells me what follows". A3's rings gave "more to look at, no more information".
- **Watch.** On all eight, "none shows what comes after". B2-Watch was "the best of the eight". A2-Watch failed: "I would not work out that one filled dot of three means one tap". "Standard taps only" confused her on every board; without it, "I would miss nothing".
- **List and upgrade.** "The one timer named for my job is locked." With A2-List's arrows she would "feel caught out by a payment screen". Price is "shown nowhere". A-family Watch lists say "Upgrade on iPhone"; B-family lists give "no sign a lesson timer exists".
- **Words.** Hers: "tap, vibrate, wrist, lesson, five minutes left, wrap up". Not hers: "haptic, nudge, session". "Keep your eyes on the room, not the clock" is "my actual problem".
- **Free tier.** Starting "15 min" by hand "needs me to look at the clock first, which is the thing I fail to do".

## 5. Resonance verdict

**B2: Medium.** It scored highest on comprehension and in-session clarity and "says what it does in my words". It is not High because no variant scored 5 on for_me ("none shows a 50-minute lesson I can start free") and her adoption conditions are unmet in every variant.

**Design or product idea.** Both, separably. The idea matched the problem she stated. Differences between variants came from design and copy: headline plainness, typeface, and whether the sequence is shown. Her main reservations concern the product and its packaging and apply to all eight: the locked lesson timer, the absent price, and upgrade and discovery on the Watch.

## 6. Threats to validity

- **Wrong surface.** Her preferences rest on Web and phone boards she would not see in a lesson. On the wrist the variants "look alike from a metre away", so the ranking may not hold in use.
- **Unjudgeable claims.** "I would know by feeling it on my wrist": a still shows nothing of feel, distinguishability, audibility or screen change.
- **Over-articulate.** She separated typeface, colour and ground more precisely than a real participant might, following the directors' framing.
- **Agreeable.** She conceded the Watch contradiction at once ("You are right"). "Mild irritation" and "a low one-off price" are soft; the "few pounds" threshold is a persona's invention.
- **Persona assumptions.** The drawer policy and period length are hypotheses that drive several findings.
- **Confound.** B3 changes typeface and colour together; she "cannot credit teal alone".

## 7. What real testing must check

1. On a real Watch, in motion: whether one, two and three taps can be told apart, and whether a watch on a bench is audible.
2. Whether teachers may wear a smartwatch and keep the phone away in lessons, and so need a Watch-only start and upgrade route.
3. Whether a locked lesson-length timer and an undisclosed price stop trial, and whether arrows on locked rows mislead.
4. Whether the Watch screen needs "what follows", and whether tap-count dots and "Standard taps only" are misread.
5. Whether the "pupil" (B) and "office" (A) readings recur, and what causes them.
