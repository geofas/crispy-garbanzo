# Follow-up questions — teachers (User Research Director)

Simulated design-testing session. No real users are involved; the participant is a synthetic persona. Answers are hypotheses to check with real teachers, not evidence.

## Questions

1. **You said your phone stays in a drawer during lessons and that you would start everything from the Watch, yet you named B3-Session and B2-Session (both phone screens) as the easiest to read, and A2-Watch as the hardest. Looking only at the eight Watch boards, in the order A to B3: which, if any, would tell you where you are in the lesson, and what on each one tells you or fails to?**
   Rationale: the "easiest to read" verdict rests on a screen the participant says she would not see in a lesson; the Watch boards were answered as a group ("all Watch boards: the time is readable") and need separating.

2. **You said you kept B3 "for colour" and called it "grown-up, calm, not corporate"; you also said its icon looks like a speaker, and that its headline lacks the word "wrist". Suppose a pupil or a colleague sees this on your wrist or in the staffroom and asks what it is. For B3, B2 and A2 in turn: what happens next, and would any of them change whether you wear it in the lab?**
   Rationale: separates liking a design from being willing to be seen using it in front of a room, and tests whether the colour preference survives the objections she raised herself.

3. **In the probes you said of B2-Session, "I would know by feeling it on my wrist," and your close asks for "taps I can tell apart while moving." Nothing in a still image shows how a tap feels. Setting the feel aside: after one look at each board, what would you expect to feel at each point in a 50-minute lesson, and which boards left you unable to say?**
   Rationale: the participant judged something a static image cannot show; this redirects to what the boards can be held to, namely whether the visible design teaches the 1/2/3 pattern before first use. Whether taps are distinguishable in motion remains untestable here and must go to real-device testing.

4. **You described mild irritation that "Lesson · 50 min" is locked, said your Siri alarm is free, and listed "a low one-off price" as a condition. Walk through the first school day with the app as shown, with only the 5-minute and 15-minute timers available: what do you do with it across your lessons, at what point do you decide to pay, keep using it free, or go back to the alarm, and what on the List boards affects that decision?**
   Rationale: "mild irritation" and "low price" read as polite and vague; a concrete day forces the decision point into the open without suggesting an outcome, and ties it to what is visible on the List boards (lock treatment, absent price, "Upgrade on iPhone" on the Watch).

## Notes for the record

- Contradiction logged for synthesis: the stated context of use (Watch only, phone in drawer) against the surfaces that drove her preferences (Web headlines, phone Session screens).
- Not pursued, for lack of question budget: "Standard taps only" confusion on every Watch board (already clear and specific); the A2-List arrow versus padlock finding (already specific); vocabulary ("lesson", "tap", "wrist" in place of "session", "nudge", "haptic").
- Cannot be resolved in this format: tap feel and distinguishability, audibility of a watch resting on a bench, screen behaviour at the moment of a nudge, real wrist glanceability. The school's device rules are a hypothesis from unverified desk research.
