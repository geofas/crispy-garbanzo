# Design follow-ups — teachers (round 2)

Simulated session with a synthetic persona, no real users. The answers produce hypotheses to check with real teachers, not evidence. Haptics, motion and wrist glanceability cannot be judged from static boards; where an answer would depend on them, say so.

## Q1 — "Not for a secondary teacher": colour, typeface, copy or ground?

Put B-Brand, B1-Brand and B3-Brand side by side, then A-Web and A3-Web.

- B to B3 keeps the layout and changes the colours. B to B1 keeps a violet and changes the typeface and the mark. Which of those two changes does more to move it from "pupils or primary" to "could be mine"? Name the single element.
- Cover the line "Psst. Five minutes left." on B. Does the board still read as made for pupils?
- You placed A with "a manager who chairs meetings". Does A3-Web, with a light ground and heavier type, change whose phone it belongs on, or does it stay with the manager? If it stays, what is holding it there: the dark screens, the headline, the word "session", or the order of the audience list?

*Rationale: separates colour, typeface, copy line and ground as causes of the "pupil" reading in B and the "office" reading in A, and tests whether a lighter ground does for the A family what teal does for the B family.*

## Q2 — The icon: shape or colour?

Look only at the app icons on A-Brand, A1-Brand, B-Brand, B1-Brand and B3-Brand.

- B and B3 use the same shape in different colours. Do both read as a speaker, or is one less so?
- You called A1's orange square "the loudest icon on my phone". Is that the solid orange field, the arch, or both? Would the arch on a dark field change your answer?
- Ignoring colour entirely, which of the four shapes (dot triangle, arch and dot, blob with arcs, tipped n) comes closest to "silent tap on the wrist", and which is furthest? If none does, say so.

*Rationale: isolates the mark's shape from its colour field and checks whether the "speaker means sound" reading follows the shape across B and B3 or is specific to B's yellow.*

## Q3 — Session screen: layout or ground?

Compare A2-Session with B2-Session, then B-Session with B3-Session, then A-Session.

- A2 and B2 both state the next nudge in words, one on a dark ground and one on a light ground. Which do you read faster, and is the difference the ground, the size of the text, or where the information sits?
- You understood the whole sequence from the four tiles on B and B3. Does that hold equally on both, or does the colour change how quickly you read them?
- You said B's purple block would be read by anyone near your desk. Is that the size of the block, the colour, or the light ground around it? Does B3's teal block have the same effect?
- On A-Session you did not take in "7:30 · 5:00 · 2:00 · end". What would that line need to be (larger, tiles, words) before you noticed it, and would you still want the screen dark?

*Rationale: tests whether the preference for B3 and B2 is driven by the layout of the sequence (tiles or worded line) or by the ground, and whether the same layout would be preferred within the A family.*

## Q4 — Watch screen: what must be there, and what is extra?

Look at A-Watch, A2-Watch, B2-Watch and B3-Watch running screens. You start from the Watch with the phone in a drawer.

- Before looking again: list the things you need on the wrist during a lesson, in order of importance.
- For each of the four screens, name one thing that is extra and one thing that is missing against that list.
- On A2-Watch you read the three dots as a page indicator. Is that because of their size, their position, or because no words sit beside them? Would "1 tap" next to the dots be enough, or would you remove the dots?
- If "Standard taps only" were removed from every screen, would you miss anything? Is your answer the same for the A-family and B-family screens?

*Rationale: isolates Watch density (countdown, tap count as dots or words, status line) from colour, and checks whether the confusion over dots and "Standard taps only" is general or tied to one variant.*
