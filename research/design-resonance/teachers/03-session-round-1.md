# Session round 1 — teachers (participant)

## Persona (synthetic)

I am a synthetic persona, not a real person. Nothing below is evidence from real users; it is a set of hypotheses to check with real teachers.

- **Name:** Hannah. Secondary-school science teacher.
- **Sessions:** fixed 50-minute periods (length is a hypothesis from the profile), five or six a day, in a lab, about 28 pupils, hard stop at the bell.
- **Devices:** iPhone and Apple Watch. The phone stays in my desk drawer in lessons (school policy, adopted from the profile's hypothesis), so I would start anything from the Watch.
- **Timekeeping today:** wall clock behind me, plus one Siri alarm per lesson that taps my wrist at the end.
- **What goes wrong:** the alarm and the bell arrive together. Nothing warns me beforehand.

## Warm-up

1. Fifty minutes: starter, practical, write-up, pack away. Ends with the bell and the next class at the door.
2. Wall clock when I remember; one wrist alarm at the end.
3. Last practical: I was circulating, missed the clock, and the bell went with equipment still out. Pupils left late and I lost my changeover.

## Task 1 — first impressions (Brand, Web)

- **A:** A timer app, dark, serious. Looks made for people who run meetings; A-Web names "meeting chairs" first and teachers last. The dot triangle on A-Brand meant nothing until I read the caption.
- **A1:** Same, with an orange arch. I read it as a doorway or a magnet. The orange square would be the loudest icon on my phone, odd for a "quiet" product.
- **A2:** A2-Brand is a builder's sheet; not for me. A2-Web is the first where I knew what it was at once: a watch, a countdown, "wrist taps".
- **A3:** A3-Web is light with a heavy black headline. Easier on a staffroom computer. Same riddle headline.
- **B:** Bright purple and yellow, round letters, "Psst". I took it for something aimed at pupils or a primary classroom. The icon looks like a speaker making sound, the opposite of silent.
- **B1:** Calmer purple. I see a letter n with a dot, nothing more.
- **B2:** B2-Brand is another builder's sheet. B2-Web is plain and adult; the headline says what it does.
- **B3:** Dark teal and peach. Grown-up, calm, not corporate. Still the speaker icon.

**Stayed with me:** A2-Web and B2-Web for plain headlines; B3 for colour. **Did not:** A and A1 blur into one; B I would dismiss as not for a secondary teacher.

## Task 2 — in-session use (Session, Watch)

I cannot judge how taps feel, any movement, or how the watch reads on a real wrist from stills.

- **A-Session, A-Watch:** Took in 06:12 and an orange dot. Next: one tap at 5 minutes left, read from the line under the time. I did not take in the small "7:30 · 5:00 · 2:00 · end". A dark screen would draw little attention.
- **A1:** Identical to me, plus a small logo.
- **A2-Session:** The "Next nudge" card is the clearest of the dark ones; the bar with 5:00 in orange helps. **A2-Watch:** three small dots and "5 min left", no tap number written. I would not work out that one filled dot of three means one tap.
- **A3-Session:** Rings round the time look like a target or a breathing app. More to look at, no more information.
- **B-Session:** A big purple block; anyone near my desk would see it. But the four tiles (7:30 done, 5:00 next, 2:00 2 taps, End 3 taps) are where I understood the whole sequence in one look.
- **B1-Session:** Pale, big dark numbers. The dots row is clear but faint.
- **B2-Session:** Big numbers, "5 min left · 1 tap", then "Then 2:00 left · 2 taps, end · 3 taps". Everything in words; the last line is small.
- **B3-Session:** B in teal. Calmer; the tiles still explain best.
- **All Watch boards:** the time is readable. "Standard taps only" confuses me on every one, since the line above says "1 tap". The B-family caption explains it, but the caption is outside the watch.

**Easiest:** B3-Session and B2-Session. **Hardest:** A2-Watch for the tap count; A3-Session for clutter.

## Task 3 — timer list and upgrade (List)

- **All:** Two timers I can start, 5 min and 15 min, then a locked group under "Upgrade to access". I would tap "Lesson · 50 min" first, because it is my lesson.
- **Fit:** 5 and 15 minutes suit a starter or a practical, not a period. The one timer named for my job is locked. Mild irritation: the free part misses exactly what I need, and my Siri alarm is free.
- **Padlocks** (every list except A2-List) make the locked rows clear.
- **A2-List:** arrows, not padlocks. I would expect "Lesson · 50 min" to open, and feel caught out by a payment screen. The small padlock in the heading is easy to miss.
- **Upgrade:** I expect the listed timers plus "Your own timers". Before paying I need the price (shown nowhere), whether I can start from the Watch alone, and whether I can try one 50-minute lesson first.
- **Watch lists:** the A family shows "Upgrade on iPhone"; my phone is in a drawer.

**Differences:** mostly colour. A3-List and B2-List are easiest to read; B-List is the most toy-like; A2-List is the only one that misled me.

## Task 4 — words

- "You feel the time. The room does not." (A, A1, A3): read it twice. Sounds like an advert.
- "Discreet wrist taps before your session ends." (A2): clear.
- "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends." (B2): clear; the one I would repeat.
- "A gentle tap before time runs out." (B), "A quiet tap before time runs out." (B3): fine, but no "wrist".
- "Nearly time. Only you know." (B1): I like it; it needs the picture beside it.
- "Psst. Five minutes left." (B): too cute.
- "Keep your eyes on the room, not the clock." (B family): my actual problem.
- "No account and no server" (A2): "no account" is good; "no server" means nothing to me.

**Mine:** tap, vibrate, wrist, lesson, five minutes left, wrap up. **Not mine:** haptic, nudge, session, "set points".

**Claims to prove:** "Only you feel it" and "The room never knows"; I would want to know whether a watch resting on a bench can be heard. Also "On your wrist or in your pocket."

**Remembered without looking:** "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends"; "Nearly time. Only you know."; "Keep your eyes on the room, not the clock."

## Close

**Would have to be true:** a 50-minute lesson with warnings at 10 and 5 minutes; started from the Watch with the phone away; taps I can tell apart while moving; a low one-off price; my school allowing the watch in lessons. **Would stop me:** the lesson timer locked before I can try it; taps I miss; needing the phone out; a staff smartwatch rule.

## Design Director's probes

1. **Whose phone?** A-Brand: a manager who chairs meetings; the dark, sparse look and "session" say office. B-Brand: a primary teacher or a pupil; rounded letters and "Psst". B2-Web and B3-Brand: could be mine.
2. **Front-row pupil.** A-Session: a dark screen with a white countdown; a stopwatch. B-Session: a purple block with 05:12 and a yellow strip; they would read it and start packing up early. In practice my phone is not on the desk.
3. **What next (from memory).** B2-Session: one tap at five minutes left, two taps at two minutes, three at the end. I would know by feeling it on my wrist. I cannot tell from a still whether the screen changes.
4. **Headline in my words.** A2-Web: "It taps your wrist a few times before the lesson ends so you know to wrap up." A-Web: after one read I could only say "something about feeling time"; I needed the paragraph beneath.
5. **Next lesson.** "Lesson · 50 min". On A-List I see the padlock and go to Upgrade, with no price in view. On A2-List I tap the arrow expecting it to start and feel misled when it asks for money. Otherwise I start "15 min" with 15 minutes to go, which is my Siri alarm again.
6. **Unsure of.**
   - "Standard taps only" (every Watch): I guess the special patterns are off, which contradicts "1 tap" above it.
   - Three dots on A2-Watch: a page indicator, at a guess.
   - The arch on A1-Watch: a letter n or a doorway.
   - The B icon: a speaker, which suggests sound.
