# Design probes — meeting chairs

Simulated session. Nothing here is evidence from real users; these are hypotheses to check with real people. Haptics, motion and wrist glanceability cannot be judged here.

## 1. Design notes (what is visible)

- **A — Quiet Signal.** Near-black navy, off-white text, one amber accent. Mark: six dots in a triangle (1-2-3), top dot amber. Copy: "You feel the time. The room does not." Session: bare countdown, thin timeline with four nudge points, coral "End session".
- **A1 — arch-and-dot.** Same UI as A. New mark: an arch (letter n) with an amber dot under it; default icon is a full amber field. Lowercase "nudgy" wordmark.
- **A2 — native UI kit.** A's mark and palette, system-style controls. Session adds a "Next nudge" card with a three-slot tap indicator and a labelled progress bar (7:30, 5:00 in amber, 2:00, End). Web is literal: "Discreet wrist taps before your session ends.", a Watch render, "No account and no server."
- **A3 — warmed, light mode.** Heavier Bricolage Grotesque, warm off-white. List and website are light; session and Watch stay dark. Countdown sits inside concentric rings with "left in this session"; next nudge in an amber-outlined pill.
- **B — Friendly Tap.** Lilac ground, saturated violet and yellow, rounded Fredoka type, pill controls. Mark: yellow dot with two ripple arcs. Copy: "A gentle tap before time runs out.", "Psst. Five minutes left." Session: violet countdown block plus four tiles (7:30 done, 5:00 next, 2:00, End) with tap counts.
- **B1 — tipped n.** Deeper violet, marigold dot. Mark: tilted white n with a dot at top left; lowercase wordmark. Copy: "Nearly time. Only you know." Session: plain dark countdown on light ground, a dot row for nudges.
- **B2 — friendly, grown-up.** B's mark, quieter violet, Figtree, standard inset lists. Session: "Next nudge" card with progress line and "Then 2:00 left · 2 taps, end · 3 taps". Web: "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends.", "Works on an iPhone alone…".
- **B3 — petrol and apricot.** B's layout and tile session recoloured: petrol blue, apricot accent, grey-white paper. Copy: "A quiet tap before time runs out.".

Common to all: free timers are 5 and 15 min; locked rows include "Meeting · 30 min" and "Meeting · 60 min" with nudges at 5 min (and 10 min) and end; the Watch running screen carries "Standard taps only".

## 2. Hypotheses

1. **A2-Session and B2-Session** will be read fastest as "what happens next and when", because the schedule is labelled in words. **A-Session** and **B1-Session** will need the dots explained.
2. **B-Brand and B-Web** (Fredoka, bright violet/yellow, "Psst.") will be judged too playful for a chair in front of a room or board; **B3** and **B2** will be accepted as the same idea made credible.
3. A dark phone screen (**A, A1, A2 Session**) will be preferred for a device lying on a lectern or table in view of others; the violet block in **B-Session** will be seen as drawing attention.
4. **A2-Web** and **B2-Web** (literal headline) will explain the product faster than **A-Web**, whose headline will be liked but need the subline to be understood.
5. On **every List board**, the locked "Meeting" templates will not match the warning points this audience is told to give; "Your own timers" will be the row that matters.
6. "Standard taps only" on **every Watch board** will be misread or cause doubt about what will be felt.

## 3. Probes

1. Look at these running-session screens side by side. Tell me what each one says will happen next, and when.
2. Imagine this phone lying in front of you while you chair. Which of these screens would you be comfortable having there, and which not? Why?
3. Read each website headline once. For each, tell me in your own words what the product does.
4. Which of these would you be content for a colleague or delegate to see on your wrist or home screen? Which would you rather they did not?
5. Here is the timer list. Which row would you start for your next session? Why?
6. On the Watch screen, what does each line of text mean to you?
