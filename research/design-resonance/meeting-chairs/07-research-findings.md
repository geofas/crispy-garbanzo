# Research findings — meeting chairs (simulated session)

## 1. What this is and is not

One synthetic participant, one run, static images only. No real users took part; nothing here is evidence from users. The audience profile is unverified desk research. The session supports hypotheses about comprehension, wording and visual fit. It cannot support claims about preference in the audience, willingness to pay, haptics, motion or readability on a real wrist.

## 2. Participant

"Helen", a synthetic senior lecturer who chairs about three conference sessions a year: four speakers, 20-minute slots, cards at 5 minutes, 1 minute and time up.
iPhone and daily Apple Watch; today she uses a phone stopwatch restarted for each speaker.

## 3. What the participant said

All quotations are simulated, taken from the session record.

- **First impressions.** The plain product variants explained themselves fastest. A2-Web was "the first where I knew what it was before the small print"; B2-Web said it works on an iPhone alone, "which no other board told me". B failed on tone: "'Psst.' is not how I want to be addressed". Missing everywhere: "the price; whether I can set 5 and 1; whether it works with the watch screen off; what happens between speakers".
- **In-session.** Time left and next nudge were "clear on all eight". The meaning of 2 and 3 taps appears only in the B family; "on A, A1, A3 I would have to remember it". B3's tiles gave "the whole plan without thinking"; B2's "Then…" line was "too small for a glance". On the Watch, "Standard taps only" "seems to contradict '1 tap'", and A2's circles were "tiny and unexplained".
- **Room fit.** "'Dark is safer' was too broad; 'no large bright block' is nearer." B: colour, then rounded numerals. B3: "only the size of the block".
- **List and upgrade.** The free timers miss her cards: "The 7:30 tap is no use and 2:00 is not 1:00." She wanted only the locked "Your own timers": "So the free version is a trial. Acceptable, if I am told the cost." On A2-List, "I would press a row expecting it to open."
- **Words.** She remembered "You feel the time. The room does not.", but: "The line is a good sentence; it did not tell me what the thing does." She questioned "nudge", "haptic", "session" and "earlier nudges".
- **Intended use.** One speaker, alongside the stopwatch: "I would not pay before that trial, and I would not rely on it alone at this conference."

## 5. Resonance verdict

**B2: Medium.** Comprehension 5, appeal 4, waitlist 4, but "for me" 3 and in-session clarity 3. Its lead over A2 and B3 is narrow and conditional by her own account.

**Design or product idea.** The product sets the ceiling. No variant scored above 3 on "for me" "because all eight carry the same audience line and the same timers", and "restarting it for each speaker is the same thing I forgot last time, so it does not obviously fix my actual mistake". Design moved comprehension and setting fit, not fit to her task. The rejection of B is a design reaction.

## 6. Threats to validity

- **Agreeable.** "Near enough to try once" is polite. Waitlist scores of 4 sit beside a refusal to pay or rely on it.
- **Over-articulate.** She attributed reactions cleanly to typeface, colour or block size, under director prompting.
- **Beyond the image.** Room visibility was assumed: "If the taps work with the screen off, most of my 'discreet' judgement about the phone falls away." Glance times were imagined.
- **Scope.** One persona with fixed 5/1/0 cards; all eight variants seen side by side.

## 7. What real testing must check

1. Whether people can tell one, two and three taps apart while speaking, and whether a tap is mistaken for a notification.
2. Whether chairs with fixed warning points and back-to-back speakers find a fit in the free timers, the loop option or custom timers.
3. Whether the phone is face up in use; if not, the Watch face matters more than the session screen.
4. Whether a memorable headline plus a plain explanatory line outperforms either alone.
5. Whether "Standard taps only", circles without words, "nudge" and "session" are understood, and whether price and "one-time" are needed on the upgrade screen.
