# Session round 2 — meeting chairs — participant response

I am a synthetic persona (Helen, senior lecturer, chairs about three conference sessions a year). Nothing below is evidence from users; these are hypotheses to check with real people. I cannot judge haptics, motion or real wrist readability from static boards.

## User Research Director's questions

**1. B2 first, B3 third.** I ranked the whole package, not the session screen. B2 won on three things outside the session: the website told me it works on an iPhone alone, the list looks like an ordinary iPhone app, and nothing in it would draw comment. On the session screen alone B3 beats B2: the tiles are readable, B2's "Then 2:00 left · 2 taps, end · 3 taps" is not. I let comfort and the website outweigh the glance. If the phone ends up face down and I rely on taps, the session screen matters less and B2 stays first; if I do look at it, B3 or A2 should be ahead. I do not know which yet.

**2. My next session.** The evening before: install it, run the free 15-minute timer once at home to feel what one, two and three taps are like. If I cannot tell two from three, I stop there. On the day: cards on the table as before, stopwatch running as before. I would start the 15-minute timer for the first speaker only, as a trial alongside the stopwatch, and show the cards from the stopwatch. The app's 7:30 and 2:00 do not match my 5 and 1, so it cannot replace anything that day. I would not pay before that trial, and I would not rely on it alone at this conference. Restarting it for each speaker is the same thing I forgot last time, so it does not obviously fix my actual mistake.

**3. Seen versus assumed.** Seen: B has a large saturated purple block and a yellow pill; A2 is dark with small amber marks; B1 and B2 are pale with dark numerals. Assumed: that a bright block catches a front-row eye, that a dark screen does not, how bright the phone would be, the angle from the audience, and that the phone lies face up at all. If the taps work with the screen off, most of my "discreet" judgement about the phone falls away and only the watch face matters. The boards do not tell me that.

**4. Remembered line versus ranking.** The line is a good sentence; it did not tell me what the thing does. I chose on what I could find out, and I remember slogans. It would get me to stop on the page; the plain sentence underneath is what would make me leave an email. A2 and B2 with that headline above their own wording would lose nothing.

## Design Director's questions

**1. Colour, block or ground.** B: the colour first — that violet with the yellow pill; the rounded numerals second. B3: only the size of the block; the petrol is fine. B1 is light and violet with no block and I am comfortable, so it is not the light ground and not violet in small amounts. "Dark is safer" was too broad; "no large bright block" is nearer.

**2. Schedule layout.** Two-second look: I only need the big time and the "next" line; of the four, A2's labelled bar or B1's dot row interferes least. Before starting: the tiles. Compared with the tiles, A2-Session lacks what 2:00 and End will feel like (2 taps, 3 taps) and any "done" marking in words.

**3. Tap count and Watch.** A-Watch and B2-Watch: remove "Standard taps only"; keep everything else. A2-Watch: remove the same line; I miss "1 tap" in words. The circles on A2-Watch are tiny and unexplained. On A2-Session they sit beside "1 tap" in words, so they are harmless there. Distrust: Watch only.

**4. Toy-like, loud, shouty.** B-Brand: letter shapes first (the fat rounded wordmark and numerals), then the bright violet-yellow pair, then "Psst." The mark is not the problem — B2 and B3 keep it and I accept both. B2 fixes it with plain letters; B3 with sober colour and no "Psst." A1: the amber field; the arch on the dark icon is acceptable, if still not an n to me. A3: the typeface — the heavy, tight headline on A3-Web. The light ground is fine.

## Ranking

1. **B2** — tells me most, looks ordinary; the "Then…" line is too small.
2. **A2** — clearest website and a calm session screen; write the tap count in words on the Watch.
3. **B3** — best session screen of the eight; the block is larger than I want on my table.
4. **A** — discreet, but I must remember what 2 and 3 taps mean.
5. **B1** — moved up one: its dot row with times reads better than A3's rings; still forgettable.
6. **A3** — A with a heavy headline and rings I do not need.
7. **A1** — amber icon is brighter than I would choose; nothing gained.
8. **B** — readable, but the colour and lettering are not for a table in front of colleagues.
