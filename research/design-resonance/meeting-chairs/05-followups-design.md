# Design follow-ups — meeting chairs

Simulated session. Nothing here is evidence from real users; the answers will be hypotheses to check with real people. Haptics, motion and wrist glanceability cannot be judged from these boards.

## Question 1 — colour, block size or ground (Session boards)

Put B-Session, B3-Session, B1-Session and A2-Session next to each other, as if each lay on your table. You were not comfortable with B, slightly uneasy with B3, and comfortable with B1 and A2. For B and for B3 separately: what is the one thing on the screen that causes the unease — the colour, the size of the coloured block, the light ground around it, or something else? Then say whether B1, which is light and violet but has no block, changes your answer.

*Rationale: B and B3 share a layout and differ in colour; B1 keeps a light ground and violet without the block. This separates hue, block size and light-versus-dark ground, and tests whether "dark is safer" holds or only "no large coloured block".*

## Question 2 — layout of the schedule (Session boards, both families)

Ignore colour and ground. There are four ways the schedule is shown: tiles with words (B-Session, B3-Session), a "Next nudge" card with a line of text below (B2-Session), a labelled bar (A2-Session), and a row of dots (A-Session, B1-Session). Which one would you want for a two-second look while a speaker is talking, and which one for checking the plan before you start? If your answer is the tiles, look at A2-Session and say what, if anything, is missing there compared with the tiles.

*Rationale: She ranked B2 first but said the tiles explain best and B2's "Then…" line is too small. This isolates layout from family and shows whether the preference is for the tile layout itself or for B2's overall plainness.*

## Question 3 — tap count and Watch density (Session and Watch boards)

Compare A-Watch, A2-Watch and B2-Watch, then A2-Session. The number of taps is written as words ("1 tap") on some and drawn as three circles on A2. On each Watch board, name any line or element you would remove and any you would miss. Separately: does your distrust of the circles apply on the Watch only, on the phone only, or on both?

*Rationale: Separates the tap-count notation (words versus circles) from Watch screen density, and checks whether the reaction to the circles is specific to A2 or to the notation wherever it appears. "Standard taps only" is included as a candidate for removal without prompting it.*

## Question 4 — what makes a board look "toy-like" or "loud" (Brand and Web boards)

Look at B-Brand, B2-Brand and B3-Brand together. You called B toy-like and accepted B2 and B3. Point to the element that makes the difference on each: the letter shapes, the colours, the mark (the dot with arcs), or the wording such as "Psst." Then do the same for the A family: on A1-Brand, is it the arch shape or the amber field of the icon that you would not choose? On A3-Brand and A3-Web, is it the typeface or the light ground that reads as "shouty"?

*Rationale: B2 keeps B's mark with a different typeface and colour; B3 keeps B's layout with different colour. This attributes the rejection of B to typeface, colour, mark or copy, and tests whether the A1 and A3 reactions are about mark shape and type or about colour and ground.*
