# Session round 2 — facilitators — participant responses

I am Ruth, a synthetic persona, not a real person. Nothing below is evidence from users; it is hypotheses to check with real facilitators.

## User Research Director's follow-ups

**1. Next Tuesday.** A2, on the watch only. Watch on my left wrist under my cardigan sleeve; phone face down in my bag, not on my knee. Before that, all of these must be true: a 90-minute timer with warnings at ten, five and two; no sound and no lit screen when it taps; I have tried it alone at home for a full 90 minutes first. As shown, with only 5 and 15 minutes free, the honest answer for next Tuesday is none.

**2. Which screen.** The phone screen in session: never. The watch: ideally never; perhaps once, if I lose count of the taps. I look at the phone twice, when I start it before people arrive and when I stop it. So the tiles matter to me before the session, to check what is coming, not during. If I can only keep one quality, I keep dark and give up the tiles. Bright is a risk in the room; the tiles are a convenience I can learn my way past.

**3. What I saw and what I assumed.**
- *Violet block "gets noticed".* Seen: a large saturated block on a pale screen in B-Session. Assumed: phone face up on a knee or table, lit, ordinary room lighting, someone a chair or two away. I have not seen it in a room. I would need to put a phone showing it in my circle of chairs and ask a colleague what she notices.
- *Neighbour "can read" the watch number.* Seen: the time is the largest thing on every watch screen. Assumed: the screen is lit, my sleeve is up, the person is beside me. The picture does not tell me the real size or whether it stays lit. I would need it on my wrist with someone sitting next to me.
- *"Faintly embarrassed" by B.* Seen: round letters, bright violet and yellow, "Psst." That is my own reaction to a picture. I am assuming members would see the app at all, which they would not if the phone is in my bag. I would stand by it only for the icon on my home screen and the website, not for the room.

**4. After pressing "Upgrade".** I expect one page: the price, "paid once", what I get. I pay if the price is about what I would spend on a paperback, it says I can set my own warnings, and it says the one-two-three taps work on my watch. I close the app if there is no price before a payment sheet, if it is a subscription, or if "Standard taps only" turns out to mean my watch cannot do the patterns. Next I would run the free 15 minutes at home to feel the taps. Tuesday in the meantime: the wall clock, as now.

## Design Director's follow-ups

**1. Classroom impression.** On B-Brand it is the letters first: the fat round "Nudgy" and headline. The violet with yellow is second, "Psst." third. The icon least. On B3-Brand almost none is left; what remains is the icon, which still looks like something making a noise. A3-Brand has none of it; it looks like equipment.

**2. Light and dark.** Setting up, the room lights are on and I am reading small words: light list, so A3 or B2. Mid-session: dark, so A or A3. A3's split is what I would choose. "Dark over bright" is a rule for the running screen and the watch, not the whole thing. A3-Web and B3-Web I remembered for the light background and the plain lettering.

**3. Three session screens.** Next two signals and their taps: B3, then B2, then A2. Least noticed: A2, then B2, then B3. They differ. I would put B3's four labelled tiles on A2's dark screen and drop the coloured block. A-Session and B1-Session lack different things: A gives the times but no tap counts; B1 gives dots to count but no words. Both lack "2 taps" written next to "2:00".

**4. Watches.** My objection is to where the locked row is. On the phone list I expect a shop; on my wrist I want only what I can start. Running screen: keep the time, "5 min left · 1 tap" and End. Remove the title "15 min"; I know what I started. "Standard taps only" I would remove from this screen and explain on the phone. Same answer for both. B3's "End session" is clearer than A's "End".
