# Session round 1 — facilitators — participant responses

## Persona (synthetic)

I am a synthetic persona, not a real person. Nothing below is evidence from users; it is hypotheses to check with real facilitators.

- **Name:** Ruth.
- **Role:** Solo facilitator of peer support groups (bereavement weekly, carers fortnightly), paid per session by two small charities.
- **Sessions:** 90 minutes, one block, hard end because the room is booked after us. 8–12 people in a circle, in person. No co-facilitator.
- **Devices:** iPhone; Apple Watch bought for walking, worn in sessions.
- **Timekeeping today:** A wall clock behind half the circle; phone face down on silent. I aim for warnings to myself at ten, five and two minutes.
- **What has gone wrong:** I glanced at my watch while a member was speaking; she stopped and said "sorry, I'm going on". Another time I missed the ten-minute point and cut the closing round short.

## Warm-up

1. Last Tuesday: bereavement group, ten people, community centre, 90 minutes. Check-in round, open discussion, closing round. Alone.
2. Wall clock, by turning my head. One look at the watch near the end.
3. The watch glance above. I apologised; she did not pick the thread back up. Since then I avoid the watch and overrun instead.

## Task 1 — First impressions (Brand, Web)

- **A:** A timer that taps your wrist before the end. Dark, technical; made for people who run meetings. The 1 tap / 2 taps / 3 taps table on A-Web explains it quickly.
- **A1:** Same page, different logo. I would not have noticed unaided.
- **A2:** A2-Web is the first to show a watch, so I see where the tap happens. A2-Brand is a parts sheet for a designer.
- **A3:** A3-Web is light and easier to read; less of a gadget.
- **B:** Violet and yellow, round letters. Looks like a classroom app. In a bereavement group I would be faintly embarrassed by it.
- **B1:** Calmer than B, still purple.
- **B2:** The most ordinary-looking. B2-Web shows the countdown and says it works on an iPhone alone.
- **B3:** Blue-green and peach. The calmest B; closest to the rooms I work in.

Missing in all: the price; whether it works for 90 minutes; whether the watch lights up or makes any sound when it taps. No page mentions support groups or counselling.

Stayed with me: A3-Web, B3-Web (calm, readable), A2-Web (the watch). Did not: A1 and B1. B I remember for the wrong reason.

## Task 2 — In-session use (Session, Watch)

Two-second look: in all eight I take in the big time and nothing else.

- **A, A1:** A second look gives "Next nudge: 5 min left · 1 tap". The row "7:30 · 5:00 · 2:00 · end" is small; I must match it to dots by eye.
- **A2:** Best of the dark ones. A card says "Next nudge / 5 min left · 1 tap" and 5:00 is amber on the line.
- **A3:** The rings look like a breathing exercise. "left in this session" helps; on others I briefly wondered whether the number was elapsed or remaining.
- **B, B3:** The four tiles (7:30 done, 5:00 next, 2:00 2 taps, End 3 taps) are the clearest account of what is coming.
- **B1:** I have to count dots to get the taps.
- **B2:** "Then 2:00 left · 2 taps, end · 3 taps" is clear but small and grey.

Taps: one for earlier, two for the last warning, three for the end. On A, A1 and A3 the session screen only says "1 tap"; the rest I know from the website.

The room: every watch shows a large white number on black; with my sleeve up, a neighbour can read it. On the phone, the violet block in B-Session would catch an eye across a circle more than the dark A screens; B1 and B2 are simply a lit white phone. In practice my phone is face down.

The A-family watches show a locked "Meeting · 30 min · Upgrade on iPhone" row. An advert on my wrist. The B watches do not.

Cannot judge: how taps feel, whether I can tell two from three while listening to someone in distress, whether the screen wakes, any sound, readability at arm's length.

What mattered: labelled tiles (B, B3) and the A2 card over strings of times; dark over bright.

## Task 3 — Timer list and upgrade (List)

All eight: two timers I can start (5 min, 15 min), a locked list, an "Upgrade" button.

1. First I look for 90 minutes. "Workshop · 90 min" is there, locked.
2. The free timers are no use to me; I run nothing of 5 or 15 minutes. So I cannot try this in a real session before paying.
3. Locks are obvious on seven lists. On A2-List the rows have arrows and the lock sits only in the small heading; I would tap "Workshop · 90 min" expecting it to open, and be annoyed.
4. "Upgrade" carries no price and no description anywhere. Before paying I want: the price; that it is paid once (the website says so, the app does not); whether I can change the 90-minute nudges from 15, 5, end to 10, 5, 2, end; whether "Your own timers" covers that.

"Pomodoro · 25 + 5" means nothing to me. The loop button is not for my work.

Did any list change my view? All equally: what I need is behind the lock and I cannot test it first.

## Task 4 — Words

- **A, A1, A3:** "You feel the time. The room does not." Says what I want, but I read it twice. "Keep your eyes on the room." I would say that. "End on time, without looking at one." I do not follow "one".
- **A2:** "Discreet wrist taps before your session ends." Plain; "session" is my word. "No account and no server": "server" is not my concern.
- **B:** "A gentle tap before time runs out." "Runs out" sounds like a quiz show. "Psst." No; not in my setting.
- **B1:** "Nearly time. Only you know." Nearly time for what? "Five minutes left. Carry on." I like that.
- **B2:** "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends." The clearest sentence of the eight.
- **B3:** "A quiet tap before time runs out." Better than "gentle"; still "runs out".

Words I use: session, five minutes left, last warning, the room, end on time. Never: haptic, nudge (I say "time warning"), Pomodoro, Psst. Doubtful: "Standard taps only", "set points", "earlier nudges".

Tone: A is clipped and cool; B is chirpy; B2 and B3 are closest to how I talk. Clearest: B2-Web. Least clear: B1-Web, then A-Web.

## Design Director's probes

1. **Icon alone.** A, A2, A3 (dot triangle): bowling pins or a loading sign; no idea. A1 (arch and dot): a doorway or a magnet. B, B2, B3 (dot and curved lines): a speaker or alarm, something that makes a noise, the opposite of what I want. B1: the letter n; tells me nothing.
2. **Phone on table or knee.** B-Session: the violet block gets noticed. B3-Session: quieter, still a coloured block. B1, B2: a lit white screen. A family: least noticeable. In all eight the number is large enough for a neighbour to read. Whether the screen stays lit I cannot tell from a still.
3. **Next signal after this one.** At two minutes left, two taps. I got that from the B-Session and B3-Session tiles and B2's "Then" line. On A and A1 I had the time from the row but not the tap count.
4. **Last session, which timer.** None of the free ones. I press "Workshop · 90 min", meet the lock, press "Upgrade" and expect a price and an explanation; neither is shown. If I cannot change 15 minutes to 10 and add a 2, I am less interested.
5. **"Standard taps only".** I take it to mean no one-two-three pattern, just an ordinary buzz each time. That is the thing the website promised, so it worries me. The B-family caption says it shows "when distinct patterns are off". Off why: my setting, the upgrade, my watch? The A boards do not explain it at all.
6. **Headline aloud.** "You feel the time. The room does not.": someone who must not be seen checking; me. "Nearly time. Only you know.": the same, vaguer. "…before time runs out": anybody with a deadline. "…before your session ends" (A2, B2): me, plainly. What helps: "single one-time purchase, with no subscription"; "Works on an iPhone alone"; "You get one email". What would help more and is absent: a price, a line that it handles 90 minutes, and a statement that it makes no sound and does not light up.
