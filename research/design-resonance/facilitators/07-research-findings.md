# Research findings — facilitators (simulated session)

## 1. What this is and is not

One synthetic participant, one run, static images only. No real users took part. Quotations below are simulated. The record supports hypotheses about which design properties might matter and where the journey might stop. It does not support claims about audience preference, willingness to pay, or haptics, sound, screen wake or visibility in a real room. The audience profile is unverified desk research.

## 2. Participant

"Ruth": solo facilitator of bereavement and carers' peer support groups; 90-minute in-person sessions, hard end, 8–12 people in a circle.
iPhone and Apple Watch; uses a wall clock and avoids her watch since a glance made a member stop speaking.

## 3. What the participant said

**First impressions.** The concept was understood in both families. B read as "a classroom app. In a bereavement group I would be faintly embarrassed by it"; the cause was "the letters first", then violet with yellow, then "Psst." Identity variants did not register: of A1, "I would not have noticed unaided." No icon communicated alone; the B mark read as "something that makes a noise, the opposite of what I want." Absent everywhere: price, 90-minute support, whether the watch lights up or sounds.

**In-session use.** "In all eight I take in the big time and nothing else." The plan was clearest on B and B3 tiles and the A2 card; A gives "the times but no tap counts", B1 "dots to count but no words". Forced to choose: "I keep dark and give up the tiles." Her hybrid: "B3's four labelled tiles on A2's dark screen". She would look at the phone in session "never". The locked row on A-family watches was "an advert on my wrist"; "on my wrist I want only what I can start."

**Timer list and upgrade.** "I run nothing of 5 or 15 minutes. So I cannot try this in a real session before paying." "Upgrade" shows no price. On A2-List she would tap a locked row "expecting it to open, and be annoyed." "Standard taps only" was read as the patterns being absent: "That is the thing the website promised, so it worries me."

**Words.** Clearest: B2-Web, "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends." A's headline "says what I want, but I read it twice." She says "time warning", not "nudge".

**Light and dark.** "'Dark over bright' is a rule for the running screen and the watch, not the whole thing." "A3's split is what I would choose."

## 5. Resonance verdict

**A2: Medium.** It scored 4 or 5 throughout and is the only variant she would wear, "on the watch only". But "as shown, with only 5 and 15 minutes free, the honest answer for next Tuesday is none."

**Design or product idea.** Mainly the product idea: it answers an incident she described before seeing any board. Design shifted the result in two places: the dark running screen (kept over clearer tiles) and B's lettering and colour (rejected). What caps resonance is product and packaging, identical across all eight: no free timer at session length, no visible price, unclear custom warnings, "Standard taps only".

## 6. Threats to validity

- **Agreeable.** The idea itself was never challenged; a persona built around a timekeeping problem will tend to welcome a timekeeping product.
- **Over-articulate.** She separated typeface, palette and wording and designed a hybrid screen. Real participants are unlikely to attribute causes this cleanly.
- **Beyond the image.** That the violet block "gets noticed" and a neighbour "can read" the watch were, on probing, assumptions about lighting, distance and a lit screen. She narrowed the embarrassment claim to "the icon on my home screen and the website, not for the room." "Fits setting" scores rest on these assumptions.

## 7. What real testing must check

1. Whether facilitators with long sessions drop out at the free tier because no free timer matches their session length.
2. Whether two and three taps can be told apart while listening, and whether the watch lights or sounds: not testable here, and her precondition for use.
3. Whether a dark running screen is preferred over a clearer labelled plan in a real seated circle, including the hybrid.
4. Whether rejection of B in sensitive settings holds, and whether it attaches to letterforms, colour or voice.
5. How people read "Standard taps only", an unpriced "Upgrade", and a locked row on the Watch.
