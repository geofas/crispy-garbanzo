# Design read-out — facilitators (simulated session)

One synthetic participant ("Ruth"), static renders. Everything below is a hypothesis to check with real facilitators, not evidence from users.

1. **Hypotheses: held or not**

- **H1 Headline — not held.** "You feel the time. The room does not." "says what I want, but I read it twice". The plain line won: B2-Web is "the clearest sentence of the eight". Sub-claims held: "runs out" "sounds like a quiz show"; "Psst." "No; not in my setting."
- **H2 Screen presence — held as a reaction to the image, untested as a fact about rooms.** "The violet block in B-Session would catch an eye across a circle more than the dark A screens", then: "I have not seen it in a room."
- **H3 Nudge plan — held.** "The four tiles… are the clearest account of what is coming"; on A and A1 "I had the time from the row but not the tap count".
- **H4 Session length — held.** "The free timers are no use to me"; "Standard taps only" "worries me".
- **H5 Mark — held, one part untested.** Dot and arcs: "a speaker or alarm, something that makes a noise". A1: "a doorway or a magnet". B1: "tells me nothing". A's triangle: "bowling pins or a loading sign"; whether it reads as a count after explanation was not asked.
- **H6 Reassurance copy — partly held.** "Works on an iPhone alone" and the one-time purchase line helped; "server is not my concern". Palette and ground were not minor: B scored 1 on fit.

2. **What drove resonance**

- **A2-Web:** the Watch render ("I see where the tap happens") and the word "session".
- **A2-Session:** "Next nudge" card and amber 5:00 tick on a dark ground.
- **A3-List with A3-Session:** light for set-up, dark while running: "A3's split is what I would choose."
- **B-Session, B3-Session:** four labelled tiles. **B2-Session:** the "Then…" line.
- **B3-Brand:** "almost none" of the classroom impression is left.
- **B2-Web:** headline and "Works on an iPhone alone".

3. **What blocked it**

- **All List boards:** no free timer at session length; "Upgrade" shows no price or description. "All equally": not a difference between directions.
- **All Watch boards:** "Standard taps only" read as the core promise being absent.
- **A-family Watch:** locked "Meeting · 30 min · Upgrade on iPhone" row: "An advert on my wrist."
- **A2-List:** chevrons, with the lock only in the section header; she would tap a locked row expecting it to open.
- **B-Brand:** round lettering first, violet with yellow second, "Psst." third.
- **B-Session, B3-Session:** the coloured block. **B1-Session, B2-Session:** "a lit white screen".
- **A-Session, A1-Session:** times without tap counts. **B1-Session:** dots without words.
- **A3-Session:** rings "look like a breathing exercise".
- **All Web:** no price, no mention of 90 minutes, no statement on sound or screen light.

4. **Keep / change**

- Keep: A2-Session card; A3 light-list, dark-session split; B3-Session tiles; B2-Web headline; A2-Web Watch render; B3-Watch without locked row.
- A2-Session: add B3's tiles, or at least "2 taps" beside 2:00.
- B-Session, B3-Session: remove the saturated block; offer a dark running screen.
- A2-List: put a lock on each locked row.
- A-family Watch: remove the locked row.
- Every Watch running screen: move "Standard taps only" to the iPhone and explain it.
- Every List: state price and "paid once" at "Upgrade".
- A-Web, A1-Web, A3-Web: lead with a plain line; replace "without looking at one".
- B, B2, B3 icons: the dot-and-arcs mark reads as sound.

5. **Best of the A family and best of the B family for this audience**

- **A family: A2** (ranked first), with A3's light list.
- **B family: B3** by her ranking (third; in-session clarity 5). B2 scored higher on waitlist intent (4 against 3); the two are close.
- **Overall: A family.** It holds ranks 1, 2, 5 and 6; B holds 3, 4, 7 and 8. The lead comes from the dark running screen and A2's plain copy, not from the A identity: no A mark was decoded. The strongest candidate is a hybrid: A2 ground and card, A3 split, B3 tiles, B2 headline.

6. **What I would not conclude from this**

- Nothing about facilitators in general: one synthetic persona, one setting (peer support groups, 90 minutes, Watch owner), built on unverified desk research.
- Nothing about room visibility or social reaction. She marked these as assumptions.
- Nothing about haptics, telling two taps from three, screen wake, sound or wrist glanceability. Stills cannot show them.
- Scores are ordinal impressions from one source; small gaps (B3 against B2, A against A1) are not differences.
- The hybrid is untested: no board shows tiles on a dark ground.
- The free-tier and pricing reactions concern the product, not the design directions.
