# Design follow-ups — facilitators

Simulated session: the answers will be hypotheses to check with real people, not evidence from users. Haptics, motion and wrist glanceability cannot be judged from stills.

## Follow-up questions

**1. Put B-Brand, B1-Brand and B3-Brand side by side. You called B a "classroom app", B1 "calmer, still purple" and B3 "the calmest B". Point to the one thing on B-Brand that most produces the classroom impression: the colours, the shape of the letters, the icon, or the words ("Psst."). Then look at B3-Brand: is any of that impression left, and where? Finally, look at A3-Brand: does any of it appear there?**

Rationale: B3 changes both palette and typeface relative to B, so the calmer reading cannot yet be assigned to colour, letterform or voice; the A3 check tests whether a warm, light board in the A family triggers the same reaction.

**2. A3 uses a light timer list and a dark running session. A is dark for both; B2 is light for both. Take two moments: setting up before the group arrives, and mid-session with someone speaking. For each moment, which of the three would you want, and why? Then the same for the website: you remembered A3-Web and B3-Web as "calm, readable" — was that the light background, the lettering, or something else on the page?**

Rationale: Separates light or dark ground from palette and family, and tests whether "dark over bright" is a rule for the session screen only or for the whole product.

**3. Look at A2-Session, B2-Session and B3-Session only. Order them twice: first by how quickly you know what the next two signals are and how many taps each will be; second by how little the screen would be noticed in your circle. If the two orders differ, say what you would take from one screen and put on another. Then look at A-Session and B1-Session: is what they lack the same thing in both, or different?**

Rationale: The preferred plan layout (labelled tiles) arrives with the least preferred ground (saturated block), so this isolates session-screen layout from colour and checks whether the legibility gap is the same in the A family (string of times) as in the B family (dots to count).

**4. Compare A-Watch and B3-Watch, both screens on each. On the timer screen, A shows a locked "Meeting · 30 min · Upgrade on iPhone" row and B3 does not; the same locked rows appear on every iPhone list. Is your objection to the locked row itself, or to where it is? On the running screen, go line by line (title, time, "5 min left · 1 tap", "Standard taps only", End button): which lines would you keep, which would you remove, and is the answer the same for both watches?**

Rationale: Tests whether the "advert on my wrist" reaction is about Watch screen density and placement or about locked content in general, and whether running-screen density is acceptable independently of family styling.
