# Design probes — trainers

Simulated session; no real users. Everything below is a hypothesis to check with real trainers, based only on 40 static boards. Haptics, motion and wrist glanceability cannot be judged from them.

## 1. Design notes

**A — Quiet Signal.** Dark navy, one amber accent. Mark: a pyramid of six dots (1, 2, 3), top dot amber. Space Grotesk wordmark and countdown. Headline "You feel the time. The room does not." Session: large white countdown, "Next nudge: 5 min left · 1 tap", thin progress line, coral "End session".

**A1 — arch-and-dot.** Same palette, product UI and headline as A. Mark becomes an arch with an amber dot beneath; lowercase "nudgy" lockup; default app icon is a full amber field.

**A2 — native and buildable.** A UI kit, not a brand board. Three-slot dot indicator with the count always written beside it; progress bar with labelled ticks (7:30, 5:00 in amber, 2:00, End); locked rows show chevrons, not padlocks. Descriptive web headline: "Discreet wrist taps before your session ends."

**A3 — warmth and light mode.** Bricolage Grotesque, heavier and tighter; warm off-white. Timer list and website are light; session and Watch stay dark. Concentric rings around the countdown with "left in this session"; next nudge in an amber-edged pill.

**B — Friendly Tap.** Light lilac ground, saturated violet, yellow; rounded Fredoka. Mark: yellow dot with two ripple arcs. Headline "A gentle tap before time runs out."; voice line "Psst. Five minutes left." Session: violet countdown card, yellow pill, four nudge tiles (7:30 done, 5:00 next, 2:00, End).

**B1 — tipped n.** Deeper violet, marigold. Mark: tilted lowercase n with a dot. Headline "Nearly time. Only you know." Session has no card: dark countdown on pale ground, white pill, a row of dots, outlined "End session".

**B2 — friendly but grown-up.** Figtree, native inset lists, ripple mark kept. Headline "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends." Session: next-nudge card with progress line and "Then 2:00 left · 2 taps, end · 3 taps"; red "End session".

**B3 — petrol and apricot.** B's layout recoloured: petrol blue, apricot, cool grey paper. Headline "A quiet tap before time runs out." Voice described as "like a colleague leaning over". Session keeps the four tiles, apricot for "next".

All eight: free timers are 5 and 15 min; "Workshop · 90 min" and "Lesson · 50 min" are locked; the running Watch shows "Standard taps only", explained only by a caption on B-family boards.

## 2. Hypotheses

- **H1 (A, A1, A2 — Session, Watch).** Dark, low-chroma screens will be read as least noticeable to a group; B-Session's violet card and yellow pill will be read as visible from the room.
- **H2 (B-Brand, B-Web vs B2-Web, B3-Web).** "Psst" and Fredoka will read as casual or aimed at teachers of children; the plainer B2 and B3 wording will be preferred.
- **H3 (A2-Session, B2-Session vs A-Session, B1-Session).** Labelled ticks or a written "then…" line let a trainer state what comes next and when; unlabelled dots do not.
- **H4 (all List boards).** A trainer will judge the free tier (5 and 15 min) irrelevant to their sessions and treat the locked 90-minute Workshop as the real product; padlock versus chevron will not change that.
- **H5 (A-Web, A3-Web vs A2-Web).** "You feel the time. The room does not." will be remembered but not understood without the sub-line; A2's descriptive headline will be understood at once and forgotten.
- **H6 (all Watch boards).** "Standard taps only" will be misread, or raise doubt that the 1/2/3 pattern works on the Watch.

## 3. Probes

1. On this running screen, tell me what happens next and when. Which element told you?
2. Your phone is face-up on the lectern, or you raise your wrist. What would a delegate in the front row take from what they see?
3. Read the headline aloud. Who is speaking, and to whom?
4. Look at the timer list. Which row would you start for your next booking? What do you make of the others?
5. On the Watch running screen, what does each line of text tell you?
6. If a client saw this icon on your phone, what would they assume it is?
