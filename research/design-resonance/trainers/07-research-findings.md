# Research findings — trainers (simulated session)

## 1. What this is and is not

One synthetic participant, one run, two rounds, 40 static artboards. No real users took part. The record supports hypotheses about which design elements may help or hinder a trainer; it does not support any claim about what trainers think, prefer or would pay. The audience profile behind the persona is unverified desk research.

## 2. Participant

"Helen", a synthetic independent trainer and speaker: talks of 20–45 minutes and guest modules of 45–60 minutes, in person, alone, iPhone and Apple Watch, pays for her own tools.
Current method: phone face-up on the lectern plus a glance at the watch; she once learnt she had four minutes left "when the chair stood up".

## 3. What the participant said

All quotations are simulated.

- **First impressions.** Plain descriptive headlines were understood fastest: on A2-Web, "I know what it is in one line". The A headline "sounds like an advert". B read as "a classroom app"; when the elements were separated, "the colours change it most", wording second, typeface unnoticed.
- **Running session.** Written tap counts and later nudges decided clarity: "The written counts matter more than the format." Forced to keep one format she chose B2's "Then…" line; B's tiles were "the clearest account of a whole session on any board". A and A3 labels were "bunched at the right, not under their dots"; on B1, "I count dots".
- **Watch.** "Standard taps only" was not understood on any board and "makes me doubt two and three taps work on the Watch". A2-Watch's circles without "1 tap" were "worse". She would keep digits, the next-nudge line and End.
- **Timer list and paywall.** No free timer fits a 40-minute talk: "the purchase is the product". "'Upgrade' shows no price; I would not press it blind." With no price or a subscription, "I close it". A2's arrows on locked rows mislead: "Padlocks are more honest."
- **Fit with the setting.** Colour affects displaying the phone, not using the app: "The colour stops me displaying it, not using it." Dark is "what I am used to, not a requirement".
- **Intended use.** Phone "in my bag or face-down"; the phone session screen "matters for about ten seconds, at the start. The Watch screen and the taps are what I am buying".
- **Identity.** A against A1 "did not register". Of the four icons, "None says timer."

## 5. Resonance verdict

**B2: Medium.** It scored 5 for comprehension, for_me, in-session clarity and waitlist, but appeal stopped at 4 and she "would not choose between [B2 and A2] on looks". The lead over A2 is one point and rests on copy and one information line, not on the visual direction.

**Design or product idea:** mostly the product idea and the wording. Her interest depends on a 40-minute custom timer, a visible one-off price and taps she can feel; none of these is a property of any design direction. The design-specific reactions are B's colours and voice (negative) and the session-screen information formats (positive in A2, B2, B, B3).

## 6. Threats to validity

- **Over-articulate.** She isolated colour, copy, typeface and mark one at a time and ranked their effects; a real participant is unlikely to separate these so cleanly.
- **Agreeable.** She was "comfortable" with six of eight variants and gave no comprehension score below 3; the probes were answered in the directors' own framing.
- **Judged beyond the image.** Lectern comfort, bright-room legibility and what a front row sees were, in her words, "my guesses". Haptics, motion, the Watch lighting up, accidental "End" presses and wrist glanceability could not be assessed.
- **Confounds.** Each preferred session format appears in one family only. Board captions and website copy informed her reading of the app screens.
- **Scores.** Single-rater ordinal scores; A, A1, A3 and B3 tie on total, as do B and B1. Differences of one or two points carry no weight.

## 7. What real testing must check

1. Whether taps are felt and counted correctly while speaking and moving, on the Watch and on a phone alone.
2. Whether trainers reach a usable custom timer at the paywall, and how a shown price, no price or locked-row styling (padlock or arrow) changes that.
3. Whether written tap counts and upcoming nudges, not the layout, drive session-screen comprehension, on phone and Watch.
4. Whether "Standard taps only" is understood, and what it leads people to believe about the tap patterns.
5. Whether palette affects willingness to use the app when the phone is out of sight, or only willingness to display it.
