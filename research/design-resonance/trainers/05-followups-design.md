# Design follow-ups — trainers (round 2)

Simulated session; no real users. The questions below are put to a synthetic persona. The answers will be hypotheses to check with real trainers, not evidence. Haptics, motion and wrist glanceability cannot be judged from the static boards and are not asked about.

## 1. Lectern comfort: hue, brightness of the ground, or the block

Put B-Session, B3-Session, B1-Session, B2-Session and A-Session side by side. B and B3 share one layout and differ only in colour; B1 and B2 are pale screens without the large coloured block; A is dark.

- Order the five from most to least comfortable face-up on a lectern.
- For each one you would not put there, name the single change that would move it up: a different colour, a darker ground, or a smaller coloured area.
- You found A3-List easier to read in a bright room than A-List. Would a light session screen in the A colours be acceptable in front of a group, or does the session screen have to be dark?

*Rationale: B was rejected and B3 accepted on an identical layout, and B1 was "unsure"; this separates saturation from light ground from the size of the colour block, and tests whether "dark" is a requirement or only a property of the A family.*

## 2. What comes next: the format, or the written tap counts

Three session screens stayed with you: the four tiles (B-Session, B3-Session), the labelled bar (A2-Session) and the "Then…" line (B2-Session). Two did less for you: the bunched labels (A-Session, A3-Session) and the dot row without tap counts (B1-Session).

- If only one of the three could be kept, which, and what would you lose from the other two?
- Picture your choice moved to the other family: the tiles on A's dark screen, or the labelled bar on B2's pale screen. Does your choice hold?
- If A-Session and B1-Session kept their layouts but had the time and tap count written under each dot, would they be level with your choice, or is something else missing?

*Rationale: each preferred format appears in one family only, so the preference is confounded with palette and ground; this isolates session layout from written tap counts and checks whether the preference transfers across A and B.*

## 3. "Classroom app" and "toy": mark, typeface, colour or copy

You read B-Brand and B-Web as a classroom app and cited round letters, bright purple and yellow, "Psst" and an icon like a volume symbol. You also said typefaces did not register. B2 keeps the same ripple mark and you read it as a normal iPhone app.

- Change one thing at a time on B-Web: (a) the headline and voice line replaced by B2's wording, (b) the round typeface replaced by B2's, (c) the purple and yellow replaced by B3's petrol and apricot, (d) the mark replaced by B1's tilted n. Which single change stops it reading as a classroom app? Which makes no difference?
- Now the reverse: put "Psst. Five minutes left." on A-Web, dark, in A's typeface. Who is speaking now?
- A-Web and A2-Web look alike and differ mainly in the headline. Is the change from "a company being slightly grand" to "a plain description" caused by the words alone, or by anything else on the page?

*Rationale: the "classroom" reading named four elements at once and contradicts the statement that typefaces did not register; this separates mark, typeface, colour and copy, and tests whether tone of voice is carried by the words in both families.*

## 4. Watch screen: which lines earn their place

Look at A-Watch, A2-Watch, A3-Watch, B-Watch and B2-Watch together.

- On each running Watch screen, which lines would you keep, and which would you remove without missing them?
- On A2-Watch the circles appear without the words "1 tap". Is the problem the circles as a symbol, or only the absence of the words? Would the same circles with no words work on the phone (A2-Session)?
- "Standard taps only" made you doubt the two- and three-tap patterns. Is that doubt the same on every Watch board, or does any board's layout or wording reduce it? If the line were absent, would you notice anything missing?
- Is any Watch board carrying too much, or too little, for a screen that size? Whether it can be read at a glance on a moving wrist cannot be judged from a still; answer only for what is on the board.

*Rationale: separates Watch screen density from the wording of one line and from the tap indicator as a symbol, and checks whether the "Standard taps only" doubt is a copy problem common to all eight variants or tied to particular Watch layouts.*
