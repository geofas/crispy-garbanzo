# Session round 1 — trainers (participant)

## Persona (synthetic)

I am a synthetic persona, not a real person. Nothing below is evidence from real users; it is a set of hypotheses to check with real trainers.

**Helen**, independent trainer and speaker. I pay for my own tools. iPhone and Apple Watch.

- **Sessions:** association and conference talks of 20–45 minutes, and guest modules of 45–60 minutes inside other people's courses. Two to four a month, in person, 15–80 people, always alone.
- **Timekeeping today:** phone face-up on the lectern, plus a glance at my watch. Presenter view when the venue's laptop allows it.
- **What has gone wrong:** see warm-up 3.

## Warm-up

1. As above. Hotel function rooms and training suites. No timekeeper.
2. Phone on the lectern. It locks, I wake it, and it looks as if I am checking messages.
3. A 40-minute slot, no clock in the room, slides full screen. A discussion ran on; I learnt I had four minutes left when the chair stood up. I cut the close. Since then I set a silent phone alarm at ten minutes to go, and miss it when the phone is not in my pocket.

## A — Quiet Signal

- **First impressions (A-Brand, A-Web):** A silent timer that taps your wrist before the end. "Meeting chairs, facilitators, trainers and teachers" names me. Dark and plain; a utility. Missing: price, and whether it does 40 minutes.
- **In session (A-Session, A-Watch):** I read 06:12, then "Next nudge: 5 min left · 1 tap", then the amber dot. The labels "7:30 · 5:00 · 2:00 · end" are bunched at the right, not under their dots; I had to work that out. The room would see a dark screen with digits. What the taps mean is not on this screen; I know it only from A-Web. "Standard taps only" on the Watch means nothing to me.
- **List (A-List):** Two timers I can start, six with padlocks. Mine are the locked ones. "Upgrade" shows no price; I would not press it blind.
- **Words:** "You feel the time. The room does not." Understood only after the sub-line; sounds like an advert. "End on time, without looking at one" took two readings.

## A1 — arch-and-dot

Same screens and words as A; my answers do not change. I only register the logo. I do not see an "n" or a wrist in it until told. The amber icon on A1-Brand would be easy to find on a phone. The small mark on A1-Session adds nothing mid-session.

## A2 — native and buildable

- **First impressions (A2-Brand, A2-Web):** A2-Brand is a parts sheet, not for me. A2-Web: "Discreet wrist taps before your session ends." I know what it is in one line, and it shows the Watch. "No account and no server" is worth knowing.
- **In session (A2-Session, A2-Watch):** 06:12, the "Next nudge / 5 min left · 1 tap" box, then the bar with 7:30, 5:00 in amber, 2:00, End, each under its own tick. I can say what comes next and after that without effort. The three circles with one filled I only understand because "1 tap" is written beside them. On A2-Watch the words "1 tap" are gone and the circles are left alone; worse.
- **List (A2-List):** Arrows instead of padlocks. I would tap "Workshop · 90 min" expecting it to open and be annoyed when it asked for money. Padlocks are more honest.
- **Words:** "Discreet", "session ends", "last warning" are mine. "Haptic" is not.

## A3 — warmth and light mode

- **First impressions (A3-Brand, A3-Web):** Same product, light website, heavier letters. Slightly more of a consumer brand. Fine.
- **In session (A3-Session, A3-Watch):** The rings take my eye before the digits. "left in this session" is useful; no other board says what the number is. The amber pill is easy to find. Same bunched labels as A. Whether the rings move: cannot judge.
- **List (A3-List):** Easier to read in a bright room than A-List. Same locked rows, same reaction.

## B — Friendly Tap

- **First impressions (B-Brand, B-Web):** Looks like a classroom app: round letters, bright purple and yellow. "Psst. Five minutes left." is not how I talk at work. The icon looks like a volume symbol, the opposite of silent.
- **In session (B-Session, B-Watch):** Purple block first, then 05:12, then the yellow pill. The four tiles (7:30 done, 5:00 next, 2:00 2 taps, End 3 taps) are the clearest account of a whole session on any board. A front row would see the purple and yellow. The caption under B-Watch, '"Standard taps only" shows when distinct patterns are off', is on the board, not on the watch; it tells me there is a setting I have not been shown.
- **List (B-List):** Same rows, big purple "Upgrade".
- **Words:** "A gentle tap before time runs out." Clear. "On your wrist or in your pocket" tells me the phone alone might do.

## B1 — tipped n

- **First impressions (B1-Brand, B1-Web):** Calmer than B. I do read the tilted shape as a letter. "Nearly time. Only you know." sounds like a secret, not a timer.
- **In session (B1-Session, B1-Watch):** Dark 05:12 on a pale screen, then the white pill. The dot row has times but no written tap counts; I count dots.
- **List (B1-List):** As B.
- **Words:** "Five minutes left. Carry on." That is what a good timekeeper says.

## B2 — friendly but grown-up

- **First impressions (B2-Brand, B2-Web):** "A quiet tap on your wrist before your session ends." Plain, and it says wrist. Looks like a normal iPhone app. "Works on an iPhone alone, on an Apple Watch with your iPhone, or on both" answers a question I had.
- **In session (B2-Session, B2-Watch):** 05:12, "Next nudge 5 min left · 1 tap", then "Then 2:00 left · 2 taps, end · 3 taps". One line gives me the rest of the session and the tap meanings. The red "End session" looks like an error at first glance.
- **List (B2-List):** Cleanest list. Same locked rows.

## B3 — petrol and apricot

- **First impressions (B3-Brand, B3-Web):** Looks like a bank or a health service: professional, a little cold. "Like a colleague leaning over" is the right idea. The blank email box on B3-Web I took for a search box.
- **In session (B3-Session, B3-Watch):** B's tiles in quieter colours. I would put this on a lectern.
- **List (B3-List):** As B.
- **Words:** "Cool for the screen. Warm for the tap." means nothing to me.

## Closing

1. **Stayed with me:** B2-Session for the "Then…" line, A2-Session for the labelled bar, B-Session for the tiles (not the colours), A2-Web and B2-Web for the headlines.
2. **In front of a group:** comfortable with all four A boards, B2 and B3. Not B; unsure about B1. What decides it: how much colour a front row can see, and whether it looks like a toy.
3. **Mattered:** tap counts and later nudges written out; dark against bright; headline wording. **Did not register:** A against A1; typefaces.
4. **Before a firm view:** the price; a 40-minute timer with my own nudges; whether 60 and 90 minutes behave like 15; what "Standard taps only" is; and the taps themselves while I am talking and moving. That last part, and whether the Watch lights up at a nudge, cannot be judged from stills.

## Design Director's probes

1. **Next:** one tap at five minutes left. A, A1, A3: the "Next nudge" line. A2, B2: the same, plus the bar or "Then" line for what follows. B, B3: the "next" tile. B1: the pill.
2. **Front row:** dark boards, a countdown and nothing else. B, a bright purple panel. B1 and B2, a white screen with a large clock. Whether a raised wrist reads as checking the time: cannot judge.
3. **Who is speaking:** A-family, a company being slightly grand. A2 and B2, a plain description for a working adult. B, someone addressing a primary teacher. B1, a whisper. B3, a colleague.
4. **Which row:** none fits a 40-minute talk. For a 60-minute module, "Meeting · 60 min", though it is not a meeting. I want "Your own timers", so for me the purchase is the product. The free 15 would do for a group exercise. "5 min … repeats" and "Pomodoro" are not for me.
5. **Watch lines:** "15 min" is the timer; the digits are time left; "5 min left · 1 tap" is the next nudge; "Standard taps only" I cannot explain, and it makes me doubt two and three taps work on the Watch; "End" stops it.
6. **Icon:** dot pyramid, a game. Arch, no idea. Dot with arcs, a sound or contactless-payment app. Tilted n, some brand's initial. None says timer.
